> **This is a published, ready-made pack.** Download the full editable materials + ReadMe from the [Glasgow record]({{ page.source_url }}) — don't rebuild it. This page just gives you a **fast way to run it** plus a **printable metadata worksheet** to hand out.

## How to run it (30–45 min)

1. **Build & document (10–15 min).** Each team builds a small model (or copies the facilitator's), then records its "metadata" on the worksheet — *enough that another team could rebuild it exactly.*
2. **Swap & reproduce (10 min).** Teams swap **only their worksheets** (not the model) and try to rebuild from the notes alone.
3. **Compare (5 min).** Put original and reproduction side by side. What's different? Where did the metadata fall short?
4. **Bridge to research (5–10 min).** The conversation the pack is built for: *what's the research parallel?* Planning what to record, choosing formats, using standards, automating capture. *"This is exactly the metadata gap in a messy spreadsheet — you just felt it with bricks."*

## Debrief questions

- What did you wish you'd written down?
- Where did ambiguity creep in — colour names? orientation? assembly order?
- In your own research, what's the "colour of the brick" you never bother to record — until someone can't reproduce your result?

## Facilitator tip

Keep models small (≈10–20 bricks) and give every team **identical** sets, or it drags and the comparison gets muddy. The magic is in the debrief, not the building.

## Make it yours

Swap the closing examples for your field's metadata standards (MIAME, MIAPPE, ISA, a repository's submission checklist) so the "research parallel" lands for *your* audience.
